# Research and lore reference

What the theme draws from, gathered while designing it (November 2026). Book facts come from Andy Weir's novel *Project Hail Mary* (2021). Film facts come from the 2026 adaptation starring Ryan Gosling. Where sources disagree or a detail couldn't be confirmed, it says so.

## The story in brief

Ryland Grace, a middle-school science teacher and former molecular biologist, wakes alone and amnesiac aboard the *Hail Mary*, near Tau Ceti. The Sun is dimming because **Astrophage**, a microbe that feeds on stellar energy, is spreading between stars. Tau Ceti is oddly unaffected, and Grace's mission is to find out why. There he meets **Rocky**, an engineer from **Erid** on the same mission for his own star, **40 Eridani**. They build a shared language, find **Taumoeba** (an Astrophage predator), and save both worlds. Grace sends the cure home in the **Beetle** probes. Instead of returning to Earth, he goes with Rocky to Erid, where he ends up teaching young Eridians.

In the film's ending, Grace lives on Erid inside an Eridian-built biodome, complete with a beach and ocean water. One review instead says he crash-lands the *Hail Mary* and uses its engines for a makeshift dome, so the details vary by source. The beach scenes were filmed at Durdle Door.

## Erid

- **Orbit and size:** about a fifth of Earth's distance from its star, with a year of just over 42 Earth days. A super-Earth of about 8.5 Earth masses and twice Earth's diameter, with roughly **2× Earth gravity**.
- **Day and magnetism:** a day lasts about **5.1 hours**. Its magnetic field is at least 25 times Earth's.
- **Atmosphere:** mostly ammonia and about **29× as thick** as Earth's (one source says 28×), at around **210 °C**. Water stays liquid only because of the pressure.
- **Light:** **no sunlight reaches the surface.** Eridians evolved without eyes and get around by echolocation and hearing.
- **What it means for the art:** Grace can only live in a sealed, artificially lit dome. That's why the sky is a flat, milky, shadowless white, with faint **geodesic dome ribs** across it. The ribs are an artistic addition, but they follow from the setting. At night the dome lamps light the ribs.

## Rocky

| Aspect | Notes |
|---|---|
| Body | A five-legged "rock spider" whose carapace is made of heavy metals. The torso is roughly pentagonal and faceted, wider at the top and tapering underneath (see the model sheet in [art-direction.md](art-direction.md)). No head, face or eyes |
| Limbs | Five, and any can be a leg or an arm. Each has an upper slab and a lower slab. In your references the upper slab runs **out and slightly down** from the torso and the lower slab drops **straight to the ground**. They don't arch above the body |
| Hands | Three-fingered hands are shown in references and fan models, but couldn't be confirmed from the book text |
| Size | Roughly the size of a large dog |
| Senses | Hears and echolocates. Can sense internal structure through solid walls |
| Voice | Five vocal bladders sound notes at once, so every word is a chord and every sentence a run of chords. The audiobook pairs Ray Porter's voice with musical tones |
| Habits | Excited "jazz hands", "Amaze!", "fist my bump". He sleeps rigid and motionless, and Grace watches over him; noted as an idea, not drawn |
| Look in the film | A practical puppet designed by James Ortiz, who also voices and performs him, operated by six puppeteers; the team are called the "Rockyteers". An animatronic version was used only rarely. Neal Scanlan's creature shop built them. The body is brown stone with green mineral flecks and a strap band on one limb |
| Beach suit | In the film's beach shot, Rocky wears a clear, triangle-faceted geodesic shell wrapped around his body and each leg. This comes from your photo; written sources didn't describe it |

## Durdle Door (the real location)

- **What it is:** a natural limestone arch on the **Jurassic Coast** near Lulworth, Dorset, England. The arch is about **30 m** wide, on a small headland of Portland limestone.
- **Geology:** bands of rock run parallel to the shore. Resistant Portland limestone sits along the shore, with a band of weaker Wealden clays and sands behind it about 120 m wide, then chalk forming the Purbeck Hills. The strata stand almost vertical, which is the source of the scene's diagonal banding. Sea erosion of the western end of the limestone band made the arch.
- **Neighbours:** **Man O' War Cove** lies just east, named after the Portland-stone reef offshore. To the west a line of islets marks eroded limestone: the Bull, the Blind Cow, the Cow and the Calf. The Isle of Portland is visible on the horizon.
- **Filming:** done in October 2024, with Durdle Door beach, Man O' War beach and the car park closed for several days. The location appears in the film's closing scenes. It had earlier been used in *Far From the Madding Crowd* (1967), *Wilde* (1997), *Doctor Who* (2022) and *The Sandman* (2023).
- **Other locations:** most of the film was shot at Shepperton Studios. Other locations include Portsmouth's South Parade Pier, the Mullard Radio Astronomy Observatory in Cambridgeshire, and The Hemel Hempstead School as Grace's school.

## The film's look

- **Cinematographer:** Greig Fraser, who called it his "most challenging film".
- **Lighting:** inside the ship, automated lighting follows the time of day: cool blue mornings, warm evenings and tungsten nights. Small palette shifts mark stages of the journey.
- **In camera:** prismatic streaks and spectral flares from filters, not added in post. A mis-timed Arri 435 shutter (the "Scooby-Doo effect") separates reality from memory, and infrared imagery recurs.
- **Little green screen:** the ship was a practical set.
- **The Erid beach frame:** the real Durdle Door, regraded so the chalk and limestone read as **jade-green and rust strata**, with a hazy, shadowless white sky, a gravel beach, teal water and an angular charcoal-timber house beside a bleached tree on the hill.
